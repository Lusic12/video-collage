TL;DR

One video in, one shape-aware collage out.

Na-V2C formulates video summarization as constructing a single structured collage summary. Instead of selecting keyframes and arranging them independently, it defines frame utility by contribution to the final composed collage: scene-aware segmentation, MAPPS-guided PPO selection, foreground event-support extraction, and shape-constrained event-aware layout are coupled to preserve narrative progression, visual expressiveness, and spatial coherence.

Abstract

Abstract

Classical video summarization usually outputs isolated keyframes or short skims. These preserve semantic coverage, but they rarely provide a single holistic visual artifact that communicates the narrative at a glance. Na-V2C introduces narrative-aware video-to-collage generation, where the pipeline is organized around the quality of the final collage summary rather than isolated frame importance.

The framework first segments the video into coherent scenes with TransNet V2. Each frame is represented by semantic features together with anime-specific aesthetic attributes from MAPPS (Multi-Attribute Prompted Preference Scoring), and a dual-head policy trained with Proximal Policy Optimization balances representativeness, temporal diversity, and perceptual quality.

Selected frames are converted into compact foreground event supports and arranged within an arbitrary target silhouette by a shape-constrained, event-aware layout that preserves chronological readability and foreground integrity. The result is a compact visual summary that communicates what happened, in what order, and through which defining visual moments.

Method

A coupled selection → abstraction → layout pipeline

Rather than optimizing selection, abstraction, and layout independently, we connect them through a single collage-oriented view of summarization. The framework unifies four stages.

1

Scene-aware structuring

TransNet V2 segments the video into semantically coherent scenes, with multi-parameter sampling for robustness to varied pacing.

2

MAPPS aesthetics

A multi-prompt CLIP-IQA module scores six anime attributes — sharpness, colorfulness, brightness, sakuga, cinematic, expression.

3

RL keyframe selection

A dual-head PPO policy with state-dependent gating balances narrative coverage and aesthetic saliency over the whole summary horizon.

4

Shape-aware layout

Foreground event supports are arranged inside an arbitrary silhouette via an event-aware Voronoi optimization with chronology-preserving rendering.

Pipeline overview. (I) Scene-aware temporal structuring with TransNet V2. (II) MAPPS computes anime-specific aesthetic attributes qt while a visual encoder extracts frame features vt. (III) The fused descriptor is processed by a shared Transformer + BiLSTM backbone; a dual-head policy with gating produces the fused policy πfused for selection under PPO. (IV) Selected keyframes become foreground event supports and are arranged via shape-constrained layout generation.

Evaluation data overview. Supplementary visual summary of the curated qualitative clips used for end-to-end analysis, covering animation, stylized, and out-of-domain video cases.

Event-aware shape-constrained layout

Video-derived keyframes impose a chronological constraint that generic collage / packing methods ignore. We formulate an Event-Aware Generative Layout: topology-aware site initialization (skeleton anchoring + distance-transform filling), gradient-based optimization of a weighted power-diagram (Voronoi) layout under core–shell priors and event-support constraints, and chronology-preserving composition via saliency-guided thin-plate-spline warping.

Layout construction. From the target mask we compute a medial axis, seed topology-aware sites, optimize an event-aware weighted Voronoi partition, and render keyframes into the optimized cells.

Layout-component ablation. Removing a single design choice degrades the collage: dropping the skeleton init, MAPPS aesthetics, chronology assignment, or the feasibility loss each hurts a distinct aspect of the final result.

  • Scene segmentation. TransNet V2 with adaptive durations gives the best balance for aesthetic selection; very short segments lose temporal context while overly long ones dilute visual focus.
  • MAPPS integration. Combining aesthetic state conditioning and reward shaping beats either route alone — the agent needs both to observe and to be incentivized toward aesthetics.
  • Prompt ensemble. Three prompt pairs per attribute maximize aesthetic ranking (MPR 0.820, Top10 0.660) at moderate cost, stabilizing single-prompt sensitivity.
  • Layout losses. The full model keeps overlap and saliency loss at zero while preserving the best balance of area allocation, aspect compatibility, and renderable cell regularity.
User Study

User-study protocol reported in the paper

The current manuscript reports the questionnaire design and evaluation tasks rather than a condensed leaderboard figure. The study separates end-to-end video-collage judgment from controlled MPEG-7 layout judgment.

Two questionnaire formats. One evaluates full video-to-collage summaries from source clips; the other isolates shape-preservation quality under controlled target masks.

Format 1: video-based evaluation. Across 9 clips, participants watch the source clip, rate event coverage of the selected keyframes (Q1), choose a preferred collage among randomized A/B/C/D outputs (Q2), and score each option from 1 to 5 as a visual summary (Q3).

Format 2: MPEG-7 layout evaluation. Across 3 target masks, participants inspect a target silhouette with four collage outputs, choose the layout that best preserves the target shape (Q4), and score each option from 1 to 5 (Q5).

In both formats, output order is randomized per case and displayed collage sizes are matched to reduce position and scale bias.

The paper-side summary table reports 27 question blocks for the video-based study and 6 question blocks for the MPEG-7 layout study.

Did You Know?

"Sakuga" — the frames worth keeping

In animation fandom, sakuga (作画) refers to moments of exceptionally fluid, expressive, or technically dazzling animation. These peaks carry much of a clip's narrative and emotional weight, yet generic diversity–representativeness summarizers — tuned for pixel fidelity, not artistic impact — routinely miss them. MAPPS injects an explicit sakuga attribute (alongside cinematic composition and character expression) directly into the RL policy, so the selector is rewarded for keeping the frames a human editor would actually choose for a poster.
